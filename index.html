5 000 ft

The handoff

The flag goes at the end of the message, and it names the CLI you want. That is the whole syntax. It works from any of the three, to any of the other two.

fix the failing test in internal/foo /claude
refactor this module /agy
summarize the last ten commits /cursor-agent

You stay where you are

No second window, no re-explaining the task. Glider runs the other CLI for you and brings its final answer back into the session you were already in.

Its permission prompts reach you

When the other CLI stops for a permission, Glider hands you the question with a token. Answer it and the run resumes with just that grant — no blanket skip-permissions flag.

4472bef1 /claude:allow

The task arrives with context

The delegate gets the task, the right working directory, and your recent instructions from the calling session — so it does not start cold and guess.

4 000 ft

Why run more than one

Because they are genuinely different tools, and the differences are the point.

  • STRENGTHS

    Each is better at something

    One is stronger at a long refactor, another at a quick grep-and-fix, another at reading an unfamiliar repo. Picking per task beats picking once and living with it.

  • BILLING

    You already pay for several

    A subscription you are not using is wasted. Send work to whichever plan has room, instead of burning one quota while another sits idle.

  • SAFETY

    Delegation without lowering the bar

    Handing a task to a headless CLI usually means skipping its permission prompts entirely. Here they are relayed to you and granted one at a time.

  • LOCK-IN

    Adding a fourth is a small job

    One YAML entry and one adapter. Nothing in the routing or delegation core branches on which CLI is talking, so a new tool does not mean a rewrite. How that boundary works.

3 000 ft

Two ways in

You do not have to choose up front. Start with the gateway; switch later if a CLI ignores proxy settings.

Gateway

Point the CLI at Glider with one environment variable or base-URL setting. Explicit, easy to reason about, works with every CLI.

http://127.0.0.1:8080/v1

Transparent

Glider redirects the CLI's outbound HTTPS at the OS level. Nothing to configure, and it works on a session that is already open.

Needs Administrator on Windows or root on Linux, and it matches by process name across the whole machine. Read how interception works before you turn it on.

Land it

Pure local mode. No cloud key needed, and nothing leaves the machine. 

# 1 — a local model
ollama serve
ollama pull qwen2.5-coder:14b

# 2 — Glider
.\glider.exe --config configs\glider.local.yaml

# dashboard  http://127.0.0.1:8081
# gateway    http://127.0.0.1:8080/v1

Then point your CLI at the gateway — or turn on transparent mode and skip that step. The full walkthrough.
